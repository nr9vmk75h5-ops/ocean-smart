UNDERSTANDING RIPS

KNOW WHAT TO LOOK FOR

SECTION 01

WHAT IS A RIP CURRENT?

A rip current is a strong current of water that flows away from the shore. It can carry swimmers away from the beach and into deeper water.

Rips are one of the most common and dangerous hazards at Australian beaches. They can be difficult to recognise and can change shape and position.

HOW DOES A RIP FORM?

Waves push water towards the beach. That water can flow back out through a narrower channel, creating a rip current.

HOW CAN YOU RECOGNISE A RIP?

Darker Water

Darker or deeper-looking water.

Fewer Waves

An area where fewer waves are breaking.

Sandy Water

Brown or sandy-coloured water.

Moving Debris

Foam, seaweed or debris moving away from the shore.

Different Water

Water that looks different from the surrounding surf.

IMPORTANT: Not every rip has all these signs. Never assume an area is safe just because you cannot see a rip.

REMEMBER

Rip currents can change position and may be difficult to see. Always ask a lifeguard if you are unsure about the water conditions.
